What does the multi-line audit desk actually do?
Start with five evidence groups:

Policy evidence: Identify the lines currently held with the agency.
Household evidence: Review address, dwelling, drivers, vehicles, and household relationships.
Timing evidence: Check renewals, recent endorsements, new vehicles, and address changes.
Relationship evidence: Review CRM notes, past conversations, and unanswered follow-ups.
Outcome evidence: Record appointments, producer reviews, quotes, issued policies, and declined opportunities.

Then perform a three-way reconciliation. Compare the account record in eAgent, policy status in Apex, and contact activity in the agency CRM. Resolve mismatched policy lines before sending any account to a producer.

The compliance-desk workflow
Keep the role separation clear. A non-licensed VA may organize records, prepare approved materials, invite a client to an insurance review, schedule appointments, and update administrative notes.

A licensed producer must explain coverage, discuss limits, recommend products, present quotes, negotiate terms, bind coverage, and complete any activity that qualifies as selling, soliciting, or negotiating insurance. The NAIC producer licensing guidance provides the governing framework, but confirm requirements with your state department of insurance and Farmers compliance contacts.
